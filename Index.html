📖 Chapter 1 Exercise

Important questions with easy answers and solutions.

❓ Multiple Choice Questions

Q1. When a copper rod is heated, what happens to its length?
A) Remains same
B) Increases
C) Decreases
D) Becomes double
Answer: B — Increases
Q2. Increase in length per unit original length for each °C rise is called:
A) Cubic expansion
B) Volume expansion
C) Linear expansion coefficient
D) Mass expansion
Answer: C
Q3. When a liquid changes into gas, the process is called:
A) Freezing
B) Condensation
C) Evaporation
D) Melting
Answer: C — Evaporation
Q4. Which property tells how much heat a solid can absorb before its temperature changes significantly?
A) Density
B) Specific heat capacity
C) Colour
D) Hardness
Answer: B
Q5. High-temperature superconducting materials are commonly based on:
A) Pure metals
B) Organic compounds
C) Ceramic materials
D) Liquid crystals
Answer: C — Ceramic materials

📝 Short Answer Questions

Q1. What factors affect thermal expansion of a solid?
Ans: Material, original size and change in temperature.
Q2. What is latent heat of fusion?
Ans: Heat required to change a unit mass of solid into liquid at its melting point without temperature change.
Q3. Why do different materials have different thermal expansion?
Ans: Because different materials have different coefficients of thermal expansion.
Q4. How does evaporation produce cooling?
Ans: High-energy particles escape from the liquid surface, so the remaining liquid becomes cooler.
Q5. What is latent heat of vaporization?
Ans: Heat required to change unit mass of liquid into vapour at constant temperature.

✍️ Constructed Response

Q1. Why are expansion joints provided in bridges?
Ans: They provide space for expansion and contraction caused by temperature changes.
Q2. Two liquids receive the same heat, but one heats faster. What does this tell us?
Ans: The liquid that heats faster has a lower specific heat capacity, if the other conditions are the same.
Q3. Why does a liquid evaporate faster in dry air?
Ans: Dry air contains less water vapour, so more molecules can escape from the liquid surface.
Q4. Why does ice stay at the same temperature while melting?
Ans: The supplied heat is used as latent heat to change ice into water.
Q5. Why do electric transmission wires sag in summer and tighten in winter?
Ans: Wires expand when heated and contract when cooled.

⭐ Comprehensive Questions

Q1. Compare thermal expansion in solids, liquids and gases.
Ans: Solids generally expand the least, liquids more, and gases the most when heated.
Q2. Give practical examples of thermal expansion.
Ans: Expansion joints in bridges and gaps in railway tracks allow materials to expand and contract safely.
Q3. What is latent heat and why is it important?
Ans: Latent heat changes the state of a substance without changing its temperature. It is important during melting and vaporization.
Q4. Why do solids, liquids and gases expand when heated?
Ans: Heating increases the energy and average separation of their particles, causing expansion.
Q5. Why does water evaporate faster from a wide shallow dish?
Ans: A wide shallow dish has a larger exposed surface area, so more molecules can escape.

🔢 Numerical Problems

Q1. A 1 m metal rod expands by 0.02 m when heated from 20°C to 120°C. Find its coefficient of linear expansion.
ΔT = 120 − 20 = 100°C

α = ΔL / (LΔT)
α = 0.02 / (1 × 100)
Answer = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ °C⁻¹
Q2. A 1 litre container of water is heated from 20°C to 80°C. If β = 2.1 × 10⁻⁴ °C⁻¹, find its final volume.
ΔT = 60°C
ΔV = VβΔT
ΔV = 1 × 2.1×10⁻⁴ × 60
ΔV = 0.0126 L

Final volume = 1.0126 L
Q3. A steel rod is 2 m long at 20°C. If α = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ °C⁻¹, find its length at 100°C.
ΔT = 80°C
ΔL = LαΔT
ΔL = 2 × 1.2×10⁻⁵ × 80
ΔL = 0.00192 m

Final length = 2.00192 m
Q4. A steel bridge originally 100 m long expands by 5 cm. Find the temperature change. α = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ °C⁻¹.
ΔL = 5 cm = 0.05 m

ΔT = ΔL/(Lα)
ΔT = 0.05/(100 × 1.2×10⁻⁵)
Answer ≈ 41.7°C
Q5. Find the heat needed to raise 2 kg of iron from 20°C to 100°C. Specific heat = 450 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.
ΔT = 80°C
Q = mcΔT
Q = 2 × 450 × 80
Answer = 72,000 J = 72 kJ
Q6. Find the heat required to melt 500 g ice at 0°C. Latent heat of fusion = 3.36 × 10⁵ J/kg.
m = 500 g = 0.5 kg
Q = mLf
Q = 0.5 × 3.36×10⁵
Answer = 168,000 J = 168 kJ
Q7. Find the heat needed to completely vaporize 1 kg water at 100°C. Latent heat of vaporization = 2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg.
Q = mLv
Q = 1 × 2.26×10⁶
Answer = 2.26 × 10⁶ J = 2.26 MJ
